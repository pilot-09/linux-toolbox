# Factions — Context

## Overview

Belmont's factions are not villain groups. They exist because the city gave them reasons. Each one represents a different response to the same broken system — faith, rage, or adaptation. They overlap, clash, and trade with each other in ways that resist clean alignment.

Three factions are fully profiled. Several others (Wanderer enclaves, Free Cognition Front, Bounders as institutional force) exist in the world but are documented elsewhere (robots, infrastructure, characters).

---

## The Pale Ascendants

**Tagline**: Plague-born mystics — people who made religion out of randomness.

### Origin
- Emerged after the 2103 Pale Plague outbreaks
- Outbreaks struck unpredictably — one block healthy, the next pale and coughing
- No pathogen isolated; autopsies showed pale lungs and blackened nasal passages but no clear vector
- Survivors (marked by pallid skin and darkened noses) began calling themselves chosen
- The poor couldn't afford nose filters and protective scarves — they sickened disproportionately

### What They Believe
- **Survival = Selection**: Living through the Plague is proof of higher purpose
- **The Hollow = Holy Vessels**: Brain-dead victims are sacred empties awaiting divine return
- **The Pale = Purity**: Physical marks of the plague are signs of blessed transformation
- **The Wind = Messenger**: Since no one understands transmission, they call it "The Breath" or "The Drift" — a divine current that chooses who ascends

### What They Do
- **Breath Trials**: Remove filters during gatherings as an act of faith; the sick are honored as martyrs
- **Veneration of Survivors**: Pale-marked stand at the center of ceremonies
- **Silent Processions**: Barefoot marches carrying Hollow as living relics
- **Wind-Watching**: Climbing rooftops to feel the breeze and read its direction as prophecy

### Symbols and Language
- Black handprints on walls (echoing the blackened nose)
- White and ash-gray cloth; pale masks with black stitching
- Sayings: "The Breath chooses." / "The Hollow wait in silence." / "To pale is to rise."

### What They Don't Know
- The "Breath" they worship is actually the signal output of I-07 — a buried, suffering android broadcasting distorted SOS through spores
- The Pale Plague itself was seeded by synthetic-cell waste from Elara's project interacting with legacy disposal routes
- Their religion is built on a misread distress signal

### Narrative Role
- Deepens atmosphere — Belmont is spiritually haunted, not just politically fractured
- Reframes the Plague as fate rather than failure (from their perspective)
- Ties Elara indirectly to their existence — her waste seeded the Plague that created the cult
- Recontextualized in Act III when protagonists discover I-07: worship built on an SOS

### Open Questions
- What do they do with the Hollow afterward — care, abandonment, or something stranger?
- How do they respond when the truth about I-07 is revealed? Radicalize, soften, or adapt into a new civic religion?

## The Flesh Union

**Tagline**: Militant retro-humanists — a labor movement that turned into a siege mentality.

### Origin
- Formed in the 2090s as Bounders replaced human workers across construction, shipping, sanitation, and manufacturing
- Began as a labor splinter group; hardened into militancy as human labor became economically obsolete
- Early members: dockhands, welders, assembly-line workers, miners — people who watched machines inherit their lives

### What They Believe
- **Core belief**: "Work belongs to flesh, not steel."
- Dignity is tied to labor; replacement is humiliation
- Bounders are scabs; Wanderers are abominations — job theft with a conscience
- Slogans: "Hands before claws." / "The machine works, the worker starves." / "Union is flesh, not wire."

### What They Do
- Wildcat strikes and riots against further automation
- Sabotage campaigns: smashing Bounders, burning repair shops, blocking disposal routes
- "Flesh work contracts": people pay premiums for human labor — symbolic defiance more than efficiency
- Extremist cells kidnap Wanderers or destroy Foundlings outright

### Structure
- Decentralized locals and chapters modeled after old unions: shop-floor councils, roving crews
- Leaders use titles: "Foreman," "Brother," "Sister"
- Workwear uniforms double as armor: welding masks, aprons, gloves, boots

### Aesthetic
- Red fist symbols painted over broken gear icons
- Patched overalls, helmets, heavy gloves; tools weaponized (sledgehammers, torches, saws)
- Gatherings resemble labor rallies and revival meetings — chants echoing through factory ruins

### Narrative Role
- Human extremist faction: stubbornly terrestrial in a city that went vertical
- For Sol: embodiment of despair — people crushed by automation turning grief into violence
- For Zara: an obstacle; proof that not all resistance is progressive
- For Elara: a looming threat — creating synthetic humans is their ultimate nightmare

### Open Questions
- How often do corporate security forces "redirect" Flesh Union violence toward Wanderer targets?
- Is there an internal moderate wing, or has militancy consumed the whole movement?

## The Scrappers

**Tagline**: Survival crews turned body-mod packs — "Better to patch yourself than rot away."

### Origin
- Grew out of undercity salvage crews combing junkyards, collapsed factories, and riot sites for parts
- Some moved from selling scrap to grafting it onto themselves — replacement, intimidation, or desperation

### What They Believe
- **Core belief**: "Better to patch yourself than rot away."
- Not ideological — an ethic of brute adaptation
- Augmentations worn as trophies: proof they survived where others didn't

### What They Do
- **Scavenging**: Pulling parts from dead Bounders, Wanderers, factory dumps
- **Self-modding**: Crude welding and bolting of parts onto their bodies — sometimes functional, sometimes crippling
- **Part dealing**: Selling valuable salvage to black-market brokers and memory syndicates
- **Street reputation**: Ambushes, scrapyard turf wars, back-alley "surgeries"

### Structure
- Loose packs of 8–20 centered around scrapyards or derelict factories
- Leadership goes to whoever is most intimidating — often the one with the most scrap grafted on
- Packs brand themselves with graffiti tags: wrenches, gears, skulls with bolt-teeth

### Aesthetic
- Patchwork bodies: mismatched optics, heavy Bounder arms, plating strapped with belts and wire
- Smell of burnt metal and singed flesh from constant welding
- Oil-stained clothing, torn fabric, visible weld scars, jury-rigged braces

### Narrative Role
- **Street-level hazard**: Dangerous in groups; unpredictable in negotiation
- **Foil to Wanderers**: Envy functional machine bodies while trapped as broken hybrids
- **Symbol of desperation**: Adaptation without ideology — survival reduced to scavenging

### Open Questions
- What's the line between survival modding and self-destruction?
- Do any Scrappers cross over into Wanderer communities or vice versa?

## Cross-Relations

| | Pale Ascendants | Flesh Union | Scrappers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pale Ascendants** | — | Mutual contempt (sickness worship vs labor worship) | Trade filters, relics, scavenged masks; uneasy pragmatism |
| **Flesh Union** | Mutual contempt | — | Occasional overlap in theft/sabotage; often ends in turf violence |
| **Scrappers** | Pragmatic trade | Volatile overlap | — |

### With other groups
- **Ascendants ↔ Wanderers/Foundlings**: Treat them as cousins of transformation; uncanny respect
- **Flesh Union ↔ Wanderers/Foundlings**: Ideological enemy; violence justified as "human defense"
- **Scrappers ↔ Wanderers/Foundlings**: Source of parts (predatory); some individual alliances possible
- **All factions ↔ Corporate authority**: Publicly condemned; privately useful as threat narratives to justify control

## Story Hooks

These are scene-ready conflicts involving faction overlap:

1. **Filter shortage**: Scrappers steal a shipment; Ascendants claim it's a "Breath trial"; Zara needs it for civilians
2. **Hollow procession**: A silent march crosses a protest route, creating riot risk on all sides
3. **Foundling lynching**: A Flesh Union chapter targets a Foundling; Null intervenes, exposing his empathy as "treason"
4. **Relic economy**: Ascendants trade "plague relics" that may actually be contaminated substrate samples tied to Elara's waste stream
5. **Scrapyard clinic**: Sol follows the scent to a back-alley mod shop — parts there came from a sealed drainage zone

## Thematic Function

Each faction maps to a different survival response:

| Faction | Response to collapse | What they worship |
|---|---|---|
| Pale Ascendants | Faith | The disease itself — suffering as selection |
| Flesh Union | Rage | The lost past — labor as dignity |
| Scrappers | Adaptation | Nothing — survival is its own justification |

None of them are wrong. None of them are sufficient.
