Factions

Belmont fractures into belief systems, labor wars, and survival economies. These groups do not exist to be "villains." They exist because the city gave them reasons.

The Pale Ascendants

Plague-born mystics: people who made religion out of randomness.

Origin

  • First appeared after the 2103 Pale Plague cases.
  • Outbreaks struck unpredictably across districts: one block healthy, the next pale and coughing.
  • Doctors couldn't isolate a pathogen. Autopsies revealed pale lungs and blackened nasal passages, but no obvious vector.
  • People adapted with nose filters and protective scarves. For the poorest, these were luxuries, and many sickened.
  • Survivors--marked by pallid skin and darkened noses--began calling themselves chosen.

Beliefs

  • Survival = Selection: to live through the Plague is proof of higher purpose.
  • The Hollow = Holy Vessels: the brain-dead are not tragedies but sacred empties awaiting divine return.
  • The Pale = Purity: whitening of skin and darkening of the nose are signs of blessed transformation.
  • The Wind = Messenger: since no one knows how infection spreads, they speak of "The Breath" or "The Drift," a divine current that chooses who will ascend.

Practices

  • Breath Trials: followers remove filters during gatherings, inhaling freely as an act of faith. Those who sicken are honored as martyrs.
  • Veneration of Survivors: the Pale-marked stand at the center of ceremonies as proof of divine favor.
  • Silent Processions: barefoot marches through Belmont carrying Hollow as living relics.
  • Wind-Watching: climbing rooftops and ruined towers to feel the breeze and read its direction as prophecy.

Symbols & Language

  • Mark: black handprints on walls--echoing the blackened nose of survival.
  • Colors: white and ash-gray cloth; pale masks with black stitching.
  • Sayings:
    • "The Breath chooses."
    • "The Hollow wait in silence."
    • "To pale is to rise."

Relationship to Society

  • Ordinary citizens: fear and disgust; seen as disease-spreaders who want the Plague.
  • Corporate authorities: publicly condemned as biohazard cultists; privately useful as a threat narrative to justify control measures.
  • The Flesh Union: despises them as "cowards who worship sickness."
  • The Scrappers: will trade stolen filters or relics; opportunistic contact.
  • Wanderers & Foundlings: viewed as uncanny cousins--freed from control, as Ascendants claim to be freed from death.

Aesthetic

  • Pale-dusted cloaks; cheap masks pulled aside during rituals.
  • Survivors flaunt marks--pallid skin, dark noses--sometimes painting faces to exaggerate them.
  • Gatherings are eerie but not theatrical: dim light, coughing choruses, Hollow swaying silently in the background.

Narrative Role

  • Deepens the city's atmosphere: Belmont isn't just politically fractured--it's spiritually haunted.
  • Reframes the Plague as fate, not failure.
  • For Elara: discovering her waste seeded the Plague ties her indirectly to the Ascendants' existence.
  • For Corin: uncovering the spore truth explains both the Plague and the cult's rise.

The Flesh Union

Militant retro-humanists: a labor movement that turned into a siege mentality.

Origin

  • Formed in the 2090s as Bounders replaced human workers across construction, shipping, sanitation, and manufacturing.
  • Began as a labor splinter group; hardened into militancy as human labor became economically obsolete.
  • Early members: dockhands, welders, assembly-line workers, miners--people who watched machines inherit their lives.

Philosophy

  • Core belief: "Work belongs to flesh, not steel."
  • They argue that dignity is tied to labor; replacement is humiliation.
  • Bounders are scabs; Wanderers are abominations--job theft with a conscience.
  • Slogans:
    • "Hands before claws."
    • "The machine works, the worker starves."
    • "Union is flesh, not wire."

Practices

  • Wildcat strikes and riots against further automation.
  • Sabotage campaigns: smashing Bounders, burning repair shops, blocking disposal routes.
  • "Flesh work contracts": people pay premiums for human labor--symbolic defiance more than efficiency.
  • Extremist cells kidnap Wanderers or destroy Foundlings outright.

Structure

  • Decentralized locals and chapters, modeled after old unions: shop-floor councils, roving crews.
  • Leaders adopt titles like "Foreman," "Brother," "Sister."
  • Workwear uniforms double as armor: welding masks, aprons, gloves, boots.

Relationships

  • Zara / Free Cognition Front: enemies. They see Zara as betraying humans by siding with machines.
  • Wanderer enclaves: hostile; raids and retaliations are common.
  • Corporate authorities: publicly condemned for disruption; quietly tolerated when their violence targets Wanderers.
  • Other extremists: sometimes clash, sometimes align--shared rage, different theology.

Visuals / Aesthetic

  • Walls scrawled with red fist symbols painted over broken gear icons.
  • Patched overalls, helmets, heavy gloves; tools weaponized (sledgehammers, torches, saws).
  • Gatherings resemble labor rallies and revival meetings--chants echoing through factory ruins.

Narrative Role

  • A human extremist faction: stubbornly terrestrial in a city that went vertical.
  • For Sol: embodiment of despair--people crushed by automation turning that grief into violence.
  • For Zara: an obstacle; proof that not all resistance is progressive.
  • For Elara: a looming threat--creating synthetic humans is their ultimate nightmare.

The Scrappers

Survival crews turned body-mod packs: "Better to patch yourself than rot away."

Origin

  • Grew out of undercity salvage crews combing junkyards, collapsed factories, and riot sites for parts.
  • Some moved from selling scrap to grafting it onto themselves--replacement, intimidation, or desperation.

Philosophy

  • Core belief: "Better to patch yourself than rot away."
  • Not ideological--an ethic of brute adaptation.
  • Augmentations worn as trophies: proof they survived where others didn't.

Practices

  • Scavenging: pulling parts from dead Bounders, Wanderers, factory dumps.
  • Self-modding: crude welding and bolting of parts onto their bodies--sometimes functional, sometimes crippling.
  • Part dealing: selling valuable salvage to black-market brokers and memory syndicates.
  • Street reputation: ambushes, scrapyard turf wars, back-alley "surgeries."

Structure

  • Loose packs of 8–20 centered around scrapyards or derelict factories.
  • Leadership goes to whoever is most intimidating--often the one with the most scrap grafted on.
  • Packs brand themselves with graffiti tags: wrenches, gears, skulls with bolt-teeth.

Aesthetic / Vibe

  • Patchwork bodies: mismatched optics, heavy Bounder arms, plating strapped with belts and wire.
  • Smell of burnt metal and singed flesh from constant welding.
  • Oil-stained clothing, torn fabric, visible weld scars, jury-rigged braces.

Narrative Role

  • Street-level hazard: dangerous in groups; unpredictable in negotiation.
  • Foil to Wanderers: envy functional machine bodies while trapped as broken hybrids.
  • Symbol of desperation: adaptation without ideology--survival reduced to scavenging.

Cross-Relations

  • Pale Ascendants ↔ Scrappers: trade filters, relics, and scavenged masks; uneasy pragmatism.
  • Pale Ascendants ↔ Flesh Union: mutual contempt--sickness worship vs labor worship.
  • Flesh Union ↔ Scrappers: occasional overlap in theft and sabotage; often end in turf violence.
  • Ascendants ↔ Wanderers/Foundlings: treat them as cousins of transformation; uncanny respect.
  • Flesh Union ↔ Wanderers/Foundlings: ideological enemy; violence justified as "human defense."

Story Hooks

  • Filter shortage: Scrappers steal a shipment; Ascendants claim it's a "Breath trial"; Zara needs it for civilians.
  • Hollow procession: a silent march crosses a protest route, causing a riot risk on all sides.
  • Foundling lynching: a Flesh Union chapter targets a Foundling; Null intervenes, exposing his empathy as "treason."
  • Relic economy: Ascendants trade "plague relics" that may actually be contaminated substrate samples tied to Elara's waste stream.
  • Scrapyard clinic: Sol follows the scent to a back-alley mod shop--parts there came from a sealed drainage zone.
